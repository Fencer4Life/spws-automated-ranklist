Domain · Ranking model

Competition and ranking

The calendar is event-shaped; scoring is tournament-slot-shaped; public rankings are segmented by weapon, gender and veteran category.

Current domain modelSQL-owned scoringVerified 1 October 2026

Mental model

Competition hierarchy

Season
└── Event — one calendar occurrence, organizer, dates and lifecycle
    ├── Tournament slot — weapon × gender × V-category
    │   └── Result — canonical fencer + placement + provenance
    └── Registration — entry request and consent, separate from results

An event can exist before tournament slots. Slots are created when the competition structure or result ingestion requires them. This preserves a clean future calendar while avoiding fictional empty competitions.

Age assignment

Veteran category is season-relative

The canonical category function uses birth year and the year in which the season ends. A fencer belongs to one home category for that season: V0 30–39, V1 40–49, V2 50–59, V3 60–69 or V4 70+.

Invariant: result rows and their parent tournament slots must agree on the effective veteran category, except for explicitly tolerated unknown-birth-year cases, and except for a PZSz SENIOR slot (see below), whose own results carry their real, individual V-category on purpose.

Combined physical pools are normalized into category-specific tournament slots: each fencer’s result is filed under their own category. What a slot stores depends on the engine assigned to the tournament type. Under EVF classic each category is scored as a bracket of its own — places are renumbered 1…K and the slot’s participant count is its own number of committed results. Under the 2026/2027 SPWS engine the joined bracket is scored whole: every fencer keeps the joined place, every sibling slot stores the joined N, and every slot stores the listing’s category order, one digit per place. An international result (PEW, MEW, MSW, PSW) takes neither path. SPWS stores only its Polish fencers, so the slot’s N is the whole source bracket as published and each place is the fencer’s own place in it; neither is recounted or renumbered, at commit, in recompute or on promotion (ADR-105). The organiser’s results are the source; EVF results are used only when nothing else exists.

International results from 2023/24 to 2025/26 are under repair. On 1 October 2026, 175 brackets on CERT and 193 on PROD stored the Polish head-count as N, and some also hold places renumbered among the Poles. Each event is re-ingested from its organiser’s results under its own sign-off; until then those brackets score as stored.

Published result

Ranking modes

Domestic ranking

Uses qualifying SPWS event families and domestic scoring configuration. Every eligible domestic result is intended to count for the correct bracket.

Extended / international ranking

Adds configured international participation for Polish fencers while applying international eligibility and minimum-participant policy.

Rolling view

For an active season, selected prior-event results may carry forward according to the season’s carry-over engine, event links and stopping rules.

Drilldown

Shows the component results and carried scores that explain a fencer’s total rather than exposing only the final number.

PZSz national senior events (PPS, Puchar Polski Seniorów; MPS, Mistrzostwa Polski Seniorów) are configured, first-class tournament types beginning with 2026/2027: a result in one scores using the season's configuration like any other type. The ranking functions select results by the tournament types each bucket of the season's rules names, so a PZSz result counts wherever those rules name PPS or MPS. The 2026/2027 rules place both in the international pool, where they count toward the full ranking's EVF+ total; a PZSz result carried over from a previous season never counts. Rules up to 2025/2026 name neither type, so in those seasons a PZSz result computes and stores a score with no effect on any ranklist.

A PZSz senior field is one undivided competition, not several: veterans compete alongside every other age in the same physical bracket. The tournament slot is labeled SENIOR (a tournament-level label, never a result category) and stores the field's full original size, not the count of veterans matched into it; a matched veteran keeps their original placement, never renumbered against only the other veterans. Matching is deliberately stricter than domestic ingestion: only an exact name or an already-approved alias auto-links, an uncertain fuzzy candidate is held in a match review queue for an administrator to approve or reject, and no fencer is ever auto-created from a PZSz source. See Ingestion and ADR-100.

A season's Season Scoring Rules can be configured either as the original two implicit groups (domestic, extended/international) or as three independent, explicitly-named sections — SPWS, EVF/FIE and PZSz — that aggregate into exactly two publicly displayed groups (SPWS and EVF+, the latter combining EVF/FIE and PZSz with no separate subtotal). The generalized ranking function (fn_ranking_full) is wired into the public ranklist and drilldown pages as of ADR-101, labeled Ranking/PPW there; it understands both the three-section shape and the original two-group shape via a frozen legacy adapter. The 2026/2027 Season Scoring Rules use the original two-group shape. The PPW ranking counts a fencer's best 2 PPW results and every MPW result; the full ranking adds, as its EVF+ total, the best 5 of their PEW, MEW, MSW, PSW, PPS and MPS results. Each type sits in exactly one bucket, so no result counts twice, and PPW results beyond the best 2 count in neither total. When an administrator changes a season's rules, the change is refused unless every bucket admits only its own pool's types, no type sits in two buckets, and each bucket takes either the best N results (N ≥ 1) or all of them.

Each season also carries a permanent, immutable ranking publication capability — PPW_ONLY or FULL — set once at creation and never editable afterward, by any role. The three spreadsheet-derived seasons (2023/2024 through 2025/2026) reproduce what the association's Excel workbooks already published and are PPW_ONLY: this is the historical ranking publication boundary those seasons never crossed, since they never carried an official combined SPWS+EVF+ ranking. 2026/2027 and every later season are FULL by construction (the column's own default). The generalized ranking RPC (fn_ranking_full) refuses to compute a combined total for a PPW_ONLY season; a season's own domestic PPW ranking and its underlying EVF/FIE result data remain fully readable and unaffected either way — the boundary restricts one RPC's combined view, not the underlying data or any other read path.

Ranking entry. From 2026/2027 a fencer enters the full ranking only through a start in PPW or MPW. The season’s Season Scoring Rules carry entry_types: ["PPW", "MPW"], and fn_ranking_full admits a fencer only with a result of one of those types in the ranking’s window — the ranked season, plus the previous season when the ranking is rolling — in any weapon. Once admitted, every result the rules’ buckets name counts. A fencer with only EVF, FIE or PZSz results is absent from the 2026/2027 full ranking until their first PPW or MPW start; seasons whose rules carry no entry_types are unchanged.

Responsibility

Where points are calculated

PostgreSQL functions own ranking and score calculation. The frontend reads ranking views/RPC results and renders them; Python ingestion determines which trusted results enter the model but does not become a second scoring engine.

Each tournament type is scored by the engine assigned to it for the season. In 2026/2027 the SPWS veteran cup and championship, PPW and MPW, use the SPWS engine; the PZSz senior types PPS and MPS and the international types PEW, MEW, MSW and PSW use EVF classic; earlier seasons are EVF classic throughout. The SPWS engine works by the size N of the whole bracket, fenced as one listing: up to 3 fencers are a meeting and score 3, 2 and 1; a single category of 4 or more, and any bracket of 16 or more, score EVF points for the place in the bracket; in a joined bracket of 4–15 the youngest category scores EVF points and a category d steps older the larger of EVF × (1 + 0.05 · d) and EVF + d, and nobody scores more than the fencer directly ahead, of any category, minus 1 point. The result is multiplied by the type’s coefficient. How categories should be joined into brackets is the scoring table’s § 2; every bracket is scored as it was fenced, and a bracket fenced otherwise than § 2 gives is reported to the operators, never refused. A published scoring-table annex and points calculator show the same numbers.

One implementation principle: age-category and scoring policy should have one authoritative implementation at the database boundary. Python and TypeScript may display or orchestrate the result but must not silently diverge.

Across seasons

Carry-over

Seasons select a carry-over engine. The explicit model links an event to its prior counterpart through id_prior_event; legacy event-code matching remains supported for seasons configured to use it. Current-season results stop the corresponding carried contribution where defined by the ranking functions.

Carry-over windows and international toggles are season configuration, not hardcoded UI rules. Biennial event families require explicit handling rather than assuming every event repeats annually.

Admission versus scoring

Participant thresholds

Minimum participant configuration gates ingestion. A bracket below its configured threshold is skipped before it becomes ranking data; changing a threshold does not silently rewrite already committed history.

Do not conflate physical pool size, category-slice size and qualifying participant threshold. They answer different questions and are preserved separately where required.
