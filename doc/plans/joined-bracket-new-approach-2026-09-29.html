Joined brackets: EVF, plus your own-category points when a younger category beats you

This replaces the medal weights, the 3.5 per fencer below, the +3 for last and the category cap with one small premium and one order rule. Younger fencers score plain EVF. Older fencers from small categories always do better than in their own bracket. Nobody scores as much as anyone who beat them.

1 · Your rules, as I read them

  1. Younger fencers: plain EVF. A bigger bracket already gives them more points, so they need nothing extra.
  2. Older fencers always benefit, measured against what their own small bracket would have given them. In a bracket of 1–3 that is 3/2/1 points.
  3. Nobody gets more points than the fencers who beat them. I read this for the whole bracket and every category: your score is below the score of everyone who finished ahead of you.
  4. No ties with the fencer who beat you. So "below" means at least 1 point below.
  5. Small numbers. The 44/26/13/8/4 weights are gone.

2 · The rule

Brackets of 1–3                → N − p + 1                     (unchanged)
One category, or 16 and more    → EVF(N, p)                     (unchanged)
Joined bracket of 4–15:
  1. everyone:                        EVF(N, p) on the place in the whole bracket
  2. beaten by at least one fencer
     of a YOUNGER category:           + own-category points
                                        = what your category alone would have given you:
                                          K − m + 1 when your category has 1–3 fencers (3 / 2 / 1)
                                          EVF(K, m) when it has 4 or more
  3. always:                          at least 1 point below the fencer directly ahead of you
                                      in the bracket, whatever their category

Here K is the number of fencers of your category in the bracket and m is your place among them. "Younger" follows the age order V0 → V4.

  • A fencer who beat every younger fencer gets no premium. EVF for their place is already more than their own bracket would give.
  • A younger fencer beaten by older ones gets no premium either. That is rule 1.

Why 1 point. Up to 15 fencers, two neighbouring EVF places are always at least 1.25 points apart. So step 3 never touches a fencer without a premium: younger fencers keep exact EVF. It only trims a premium that would reach the fencer ahead.

W stawce łączonej (4–15 zawodników) każdy dostaje punkty EVF za miejsce w całej stawce. Jeśli wyprzedził Cię zawodnik z młodszej kategorii, dostajesz dodatkowo punkty, które dałby Ci turniej samej Twojej kategorii. Nigdy jednak nie masz tyle punktów co ktokolwiek, kto skończył przed Tobą — zawsze co najmniej 1 pkt mniej.

5 · The one conflict: older categories of 4 or more

Rules 2 and 3 cannot both hold here

A category of 4+ has a real bracket of its own, scored by EVF. Its winner would score 84.29 with 4 fencers and 98.00 with 8. If that winner finishes 5th behind four younger fencers, rule 3 holds them 1 point under the 4th: 41.66 in example D. No premium can fix this without breaking rule 3.

In the uniform simulation, which includes many older categories of 4+, 30% of those fencers score less than in their own bracket. The median shortfall is 12.3 points and the worst is 88.1. Categories of 1–3 are never affected.

Question — which rule wins for an older category of 4 or more?
  1. Rule 3 wins: nobody is scored above anyone who beat themrecommended
    • It is your explicit rule, and it keeps the bracket's order intact for everyone.
    • A category of 4+ can fence its own bracket, so joining is its choice.
    • The annex says in one sentence that for a category of 4 or more, joining can cost points.
  2. Rule 2 wins: never less than your own bracket. To keep the order, the younger fencers ahead would have to be lifted above the older fencer. In example D, the younger fencers at 2nd–4th would go from 80 / 55 / 43 to above 85 points. That inflates the younger scores this rule was meant to leave alone.
  3. Only join categories of 1–3. This is an organiser's rule, not a scoring rule, and it can sit on top of option 1.

After your answer, the implementation plan is rewritten around this rule.

  • Ingestion stores the count of younger-category fencers ahead.
  • The writer scores a joined bracket as one unit, because step 3 crosses categories.
  • The annex and calculator are rebuilt to match.
