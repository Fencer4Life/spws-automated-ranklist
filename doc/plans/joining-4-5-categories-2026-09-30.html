Joining 4 or 5 categories: every historical case, and how the rule scores it

Under your joining rules (at most 2 categories, 3 when unavoidable), 39 joined brackets in three seasons still have only 2–3 fencers. Here are the 15 cases where getting every bracket to 4 fencers would need 4 or 5 categories in one bracket. Below them: how the place-ahead rule scores a bracket of 5 categories and 5–6 fencers, checked in every finishing order.

1 · The 15 cases

Each row shows three things:

  • the real category sizes;
  • the brackets under your rules 1–4;
  • the single bracket needed to reach 4 fencers.

The last column is each category's average score over every finishing order: under your rules → with the big bracket. A single fencer alone would score 1 point; a bracket of 2 averages 1.5 and a bracket of 3 averages 2.

2 · How the rule scores 5 categories in a bracket of 5 or 6

Ta sama reguła, bez zmian
  1. Zawodnik najmłodszej kategorii w stawce (tu V0) dostaje punkty EVF za miejsce w całej stawce.
  2. Zawodnik każdej starszej kategorii, jeśli na miejscu tuż przed nim jest zawodnik najmłodszej kategorii, dostaje wynik tamtego zawodnika minus 1 pkt.
  3. W każdym innym przypadku zawodnik starszej kategorii dostaje punkty EVF + 1 pkt.

With one or two V0 fencers in the bracket, only the fencer directly behind each of them is lifted. Everyone else from V1–V4 gets EVF + 1. In a bracket of mostly single-fencer categories, the rule is therefore EVF + 1 for almost everyone, plus one or two lifts. Every fencer of a single-fencer category would score 1 point alone, so every one of them gains.

3 · What this means

The rule copes with 5 categories
  • No change to the rule is needed. With five categories, V0 keeps plain EVF, every older fencer gains, and the order stays strict with gaps of at least 1 point.
  • The premium stays modest on average (5–7 points). The large lift (up to 35) goes only to the fencer placed directly behind a V0 fencer near the top.
What to weigh
  • Five age categories in one list. V0 and V4 would fence each other. That is an organiser's and safety question, not a scoring one.
  • Big gains for the smallest categories. A single-fencer category goes from about 1.5 points on average, in a bracket of 2, to 36–50. That is the purpose of joining. The table pays 3 / 2 / 1; the big bracket pays EVF on a real field.
  • Some categories lose average points when their bracket of 4–6 grows to 7–10.
  • It would still leave 12 joined brackets of 2–3 fencers, where even all categories together have fewer than 4 fencers.

4 · Question

Allow 4–5 categories when a bracket would otherwise stay below 4 fencers?
  1. Yes, only to lift a bracket of 2–3 fencers to at least 4recommended
    • In three seasons this means 15 brackets of 4–5 categories, and the joined brackets of 2–3 fencers fall from 39 to 12.
    • The scoring rule needs no change, and every principle holds for small categories.
    • Proposed rule 5 still applies, so a category of 4+ is never an older category inside.
    • Your rule 3 would read: „Trzy lub więcej kategorii łączy się tylko wtedy, gdy inaczej nie da się połączyć wszystkich kategorii poniżej 4 zawodników albo stawka miałaby mniej niż 4 zawodników.”
  2. No, keep at most 3. The 39 brackets of 2–3 fencers stay on the table (3 / 2 / 1), and five age categories never share one list.
