Plan · ranklist display · 18 October 2026 · revision 2 (design C)

Whole points, with a decimal only for close calls

The main ranklist shows every points value as a whole number. Only where two neighbours would show the same whole number while their totals differ does the column that orders the list add a small grey decimal. The drilldown and the ODS download keep their decimals. The ranking, its order and every stored score stay exactly as they are. The decision is recorded in a new ADR-114, with the mocks inside it.

3 · The rule

Whole everywhere; a decimal only where it explains the order

  1. Every number in the main table is whole, a half rounding up (Math.round): Punkty in the PPW view; SPWS, EVF+ and Razem in the Ranking view. An empty value still shows —. Stored scores have two decimals and any x.50 is exact in binary, so a half is never misrounded.
  2. The close-call exception applies only to the column that orders the list: Razem in the Ranking view, Punkty in the PPW view. Rows that show the same whole number always sit next to each other, because the list is sorted and rounding never reorders. Such a run of rows is a close call when its exact totals are not all equal. Then every row in the run shows its decimal: one decimal, or two when one decimal would still show two different totals as equal.
  3. Exact ties stay whole. A run whose totals are all equal shows whole numbers: no decimal can explain an order the database does not fix.
  4. The decimal’s look is B′: 0.72 em, which is 10 px under the 14 px digits (9.4 px under 13 px on a phone), in the table’s grey #999. The separator stays the point the table uses today.
  5. SPWS and EVF+ stay whole even in a close-call row; they do not decide the order.

ADR-114: The main ranklist shows whole points; a decimal appears only where neighbours would look equal

Status: Draft (proposed 2026-10-18; awaiting sign-off)
Date: 2026-10-18
Relates to: ADR-101 (the table and its columns), ADR-098 (the rank the functions return), ADR-078 (placeholder names in the committed mocks), ADR-102 (untouched)
Source: frontend/src/lib/ranklist-points.ts, frontend/src/components/RanklistTable.svelte, doc/plans/ranklist-whole-points-2026-10-18.html, doc/mockups/ranklist-whole-vs-small-decimals-2026-10-18.html

Context

The main ranklist showed one decimal on every number (RanklistTable.svelte:58–62). Readers compare whole points, so the decimals cluttered the list without informing it. On 18 October the user asked to remove them from the main list and keep them in the drilldown. Measured on PROD that day as the site reads the lists (2026/27 with the rolling carry-over, 2025/26 without), whole numbers make 33 of 607 neighbour pairs look equal. In 20 of them the totals differ (9 in Ranking 2026/27, 8 in PPW 2026/27, 3 in PPW 2025/26), so a reader sees two ranks with the same number and cannot tell why; 2 of those still look equal at one decimal (71.34 and 71.31). The other 13 are exact ties. In 20 of 256 Ranking rows, the rounded SPWS + EVF+ misses the rounded Razem by one; a one-decimal display already misses by 0.1. Every ranking function numbers rows with ROW_NUMBER() OVER (ORDER BY total DESC) and no second key (20261003000018_carry_stop_results.sql:222, :508; 20260928000001_spws_place_medal_engine.sql:1220, :1337), so equal totals take consecutive ranks in no fixed order.

Decision

1 · Whole points. Every number in the main table is whole, a half rounding up (Math.round): Punkty, SPWS, EVF+ and Razem. An empty value shows —.

2 · A decimal only for close calls, only in the column that orders the list (Razem, Punkty). Rows that show the same whole number are neighbours, because the list is sorted and rounding never reorders. When their exact totals are not all equal, every row of that run shows its decimal: one, or two when one would still look equal. Exact ties stay whole.

3 · The decimal’s look. 0.72 em in the table’s grey #999, rendered as markup, never as raw HTML. The separator stays the point.

4 · What does not change. The drilldown keeps one decimal. The ODS download keeps the exact values. The order, the rank, the column widths, the points calculator and the Points Table are unchanged.

5 · One pure module. frontend/src/lib/ranklist-points.ts holds the rule (wholePoints, closeCallDecimals, splitPoints); the component only draws.

Alternatives considered

  1. Today’s one decimal everywhere. Rejected: it is the clutter the user named.
  2. A · whole numbers only. Rejected: two different ranks can show the same number with no visible reason.
  3. B · every decimal three sizes smaller. Rejected: every number keeps a tail, .0 on zeros and whole numbers included (a zero in 5 of the mock list’s 10 SPWS cells). The list gets quieter, not clean.
  4. B′ · B in grey. Rejected for the same reason, less loudly; its look is kept for the close calls.
  5. Decimals in every column of a close-call row. Rejected: SPWS and EVF+ do not decide the order; it would add clutter exactly where the signal sits.
  6. Always two decimals for a close call. Rejected: one is enough in 18 of the 20 close calls PROD shows; two appear only where one is not (71.34 and 71.31).
  7. Shared ranks for equal totals. Not decided here: it is a ranking rule, not a display (open item 1).

Consequences

  • New: frontend/src/lib/ranklist-points.ts, frontend/tests/ranklistPoints.test.ts, doc/adr/assets/adr-114-*.png. Modified: RanklistTable.svelte, RanklistTable.test.ts, DrilldownModal.test.ts, export.test.ts. Tests RT.ROUND.01–10.
  • A row’s look depends on its neighbours: a 22 becomes 22.1 while another fencer is within the same whole number, and goes back when they part.
  • Those few rows break the clean right edge of the column by a small tail.
  • The visible sum can miss by one; the drilldown shows the exact parts.
  • Found, not fixed: equal totals take consecutive ranks in no fixed order (ROW_NUMBER without a second key).
  • The committed mocks carry real scores and placeholder names (ADR-078).

Open items

  1. How equal totals are ranked. Today: consecutive ranks, order not fixed. Recommendation: decide it as its own ranking rule, either a shared rank or a written tie-break, in a separate session. This ADR keeps exact ties whole either way.
