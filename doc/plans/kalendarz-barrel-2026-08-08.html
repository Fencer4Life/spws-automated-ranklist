Calendar UI replacement — quarter barrel + single event card

Replacing the calendar's month-grouped list and flat progress strip with a rotating three-row quarter barrel that drives a single full-detail event card.

Date 8 August 2026 · revised 9 August Status Design complete, not implemented Surface App calendar + public spws-calendar embed Replaces CalendarView.svelte (659 lines)

Frontend source is untouched; nothing here is built. Start at §06 Phase 1, which is self-contained and blocked only by open items 1 and 4 in §09, both of which land in a single function you can write last.

01Context

The calendar today is one 659-line component, frontend/src/components/CalendarView.svelte, doing everything at once: season dropdown, scope and time filters, a flat rolling-progress strip, a month-grouped reverse-chronological event list, and the registration modal. On a phone a 21-event season is an unbroken vertical scroll.

The progress strip has a specific, diagnosable flaw: it encodes two variables in one visual channel. Hue says what kind of event it is; lightness of that same hue says whether it has been held. Light-blue-planned and light-green-planned sit close enough in value that the eye cannot separate "which type" from "which state". The fix is not better colours — it is giving state its own channel.

The replacement inverts the structure. A rotating three-row quarter barrel becomes the primary control, and selecting a panel shows exactly one event in a full-detail card beneath it. The season dropdown and the event list are both removed; the barrel replaces them.

02What the mock requires

  1. Quarter barrel. Three visible rows on a 3D drum: focused quarter centred, previous receded above at rotateX(46deg), next receded below at rotateX(-46deg). Rotation is translateY on the drum container; facing angle is rotateX per row. The DOM never re-renders on rotate — only classes change — which is what makes it read as mechanical rather than as a crossfade.
  2. Detail tiers follow the rotation. Focused row shows day, month and code; receded rows drop to day and code, and panels narrow from 48px to 38px. Because the outer rows are visibly receding, they have earned the right to carry less. This is what fits ~21 events into ~216px. The selected panel spells its month out in full — wrz becomes września — so the abbreviation is only ever a consequence of not being selected, never the only form on offer.
  3. Engraved seams. The quarter label (4Q26) sits at 11px muted on a hairline above each row and rotates with it. The season code (26/27) appears at the right end of the seam on the focused row, and permanently on season-boundary seams — which also take a 2px --border-stronger rule so a boundary is visible before you reach it.
  4. Continuous history. The drum rolls back through every prior season to the start of history. No season clamp. Crossing a boundary updates season state.
  5. Panels. 48px focused, 38px receded, centred via an inner margin: 0 auto flex wrapper. Height is 56px, which is exactly what four lines of type need once they are stopped from shrinking — see the callout below. Hue encodes type; fill encodes completed; a 2px accent ring marks the next upcoming event, computed from the visible set rather than assigned.
  6. The selected panel widens to fit what it has to say: 78px with a city, 74px without. This supersedes the earlier 48px when the event has no city — the full month name made 48px impossible, since października alone measures 66px. The selected panel is never the plain width in either language.
  7. The whole row is the tap target. Tapping a receded row rotates it to centre; tapping a panel on the focused row selects that event. An 11px label is a poor thing to ask someone to hit with a thumb.
  8. Filter centred below the header: a single PPW | +EVF segmented control. No weapon filter — the drum holds the whole history without compressing it. This also retires the existing all/past/future .time-filter-select: the drum is the time control, and a dropdown that hides half the barrel's rows fights it. Recording it explicitly because it is a behaviour removal, not a styling change, and one test asserts it.
  9. Header: hamburger, SPWS logo mark, view title, EN | PL toggle. The logo is the image, not a text wordmark — frontend/public/SPWS-logo.png, rendered as <img src="SPWS-logo.png" alt="SPWS" class="header-logo"> at height:22px, dropping to 18px at the mobile breakpoint. This is already how the app works (App.svelte:15); the earlier text wordmark existed only in the mock, which has now been corrected to match. Nothing to build here — the header is App.svelte chrome that the barrel sits beneath, not one of the new components, and it is listed only so the mock reads as a faithful screen.
  10. Single event card, joined to its panel by a caret positioned from the panel's measured getBoundingClientRect() after the rotation settles. Every optional row is omitted entirely when its field is empty — no placeholders, no “brak danych”. The card's full field set is the contract table in §04, not the shorter list the mock happens to render: the card must carry everything CalendarView.svelte shows today, including the registration flow and the fee tiers, or the redesign is a functional regression.
  11. 320px is the floor. Five panels lay out flat in the focused row at 320px; six or more fan and overlap. This is one fewer than the original design, and it is the price of the full month name on the selected panel — widening it from 48px to 74px spends the room a sixth flat panel needed. Overlapping is the designed response to overflow, not a failure, so the change costs nothing functional; it is recorded because the old six panels fit figure is now wrong and will not reproduce. Measured across all 19 quarters at 320px.
Results links are labelled by day, never by weapon. ADR-040 is explicit that the five URL slots are "equal-status — no role labels, no per-slot enum, no primary pointer". They exist because organisers split results across multiple platform URLs, usually per day. So: one URL renders as Wyniki; several render as Dzień 1, Dzień 2. Where a single day carries two weapons, both still read as that day — the card never claims a slot belongs to a weapon.
Two details worth protecting during implementation. First, justify-content: center combined with overflow-x: auto clips the leading items when a row overflows, and they become unreachable — the inner margin: 0 auto wrapper is the fix, not a stylistic preference. Second, the next-upcoming ring must be derived from the filtered set: switching to PPW moves it off PEW7 Wiedeń and onto MPW Warszawa, which is genuinely the next domestic event enterable. A hardcoded ring would point at an event the filter just removed.

Domestic events are typed by an SPWS admin, who naturally enters a city. PEW events arrive from the EVF calendar scraper, which for most of its history captured nothing. Fill rate by season — 30%, 54%, 76%, 68% — tracks the scraper gaining the field, not anyone backfilling. 45 PEW events have no location.

txt_location must always hold a city. Rows like Sporthalle der Städtischen Berufsschule für Informationstechnik and Savoy Terrace - Buda Castle are a scraping defect, not a modelling choice — the scraper is writing the venue into the city field. Venues belong in txt_venue_address. Fixing the scraper and cleaning the polluted rows are one job.

Rendering rule: if txt_location holds anything, it appears. Classification never gates visibility. The venue/city split decides which line the string lands on and whether it earns a flag — never whether the user sees it. A row is blank on screen only when the column is genuinely NULL.

This was a real defect in the mock, not a hypothetical. Its splitLoc() returned an empty city for anything matching the venue heuristic, and the panel rendered e.city alone — so 9 of the 56 events that have a location showed none at all. The worst case was Guildford Spectrum: the city is literally the first word, and the heuristic threw the whole string away because the second word matched spectrum. A lossy classifier had been promoted into a visibility gate. The mock now falls back to the raw value, and panel coverage went from 47 to 56.

So: 45 blank PEW locations are correct — that data does not exist, and §02's omit-when-empty rule covers it. Anything else blank is a bug. The card handles both shapes: a city-classified string takes the flag line, a venue-classified one takes the address line below it, and either way the string is on screen and in the clipboard.

Country is free text in mixed languages

Polska, Germany, GRUZJA, and both Great Britain and United Kingdom for the same country. Normalise to ISO-3166 alpha-2 in a one-time enrichment pass with human review of the ambiguous cases — never a per-render web lookup, which would be slow, non-deterministic and unauditable. Once stored, flags become a static map and country names come from the existing EN/PL locale files.

arr_weapons exists and is unusable

This is a backfill and a write path, not a new column.

Nothing will catch you if you drop it. Leaving dualEnv and activeEnv declared in the props interface but never rendered compiles clean, passes svelte-check, and fails only at runtime — as a silently missing control, which is the worst way for this particular one to fail.

It is deliberately temporary, and that changes how much to invest in it. The switch exists only because CERT and PROD are both served from GitHub Pages, so a single static bundle has to be able to point at either. It retires when PROD moves to the WordPress CMS — at that point the calendar is embedded in a site that is production, there is no second environment to flip to, and the control, the dualEnv / activeEnv props and the four tests in env-toggle.test.ts all go together.

So: carry it across unchanged and do not improve it. Note in passing that App.svelte:70-79 renders a byte-identical second copy for the ranklist view — the markup sits inside the {#if currentView} chain, so each branch needs its own. Hoisting it above the chain would remove the duplicate, but it would also add the footer to the admin views, which do not have it today. Do not do this. Refactoring a control with a known removal date spends effort on something whose best outcome is being deleted more tidily; the duplication is two small blocks of markup and it costs nothing to leave alone until both disappear.

The registration block is a flow, not a row — port its logic verbatim. Three of the five gaps share one date computation, and it is subtle enough that a rewrite from memory will get it wrong: regCutoff = dt_registration_deadline ?? dt_start, and regOpen = today ≤ regCutoff. So the registration and entry-list links stay visible until the deadline or, when no deadline is stored, until the event starts — which is what keeps them live for the 98 of 103 events that have no deadline at all. The deadline text has a stricter test than the links: it needs dt_registration_deadline to be non-null in its own right. regUrgent turns the block red when the cutoff is under seven days away. What the card leads with

The card is ordered by what a fencer actually acts on. Identity first (date, code, name, place), then a two-chip status line, then a rule, then the decision block: entry fee, the two tiered fees, the registration deadline, and the two links. Weapons close the card as small muted pills — they qualify the event, they are not why anyone opened it.

The tournament count is gone. num_tournaments is filled on 103/103 events, which made it the easiest thing to put on a card and the least useful thing to read there: it answers a question nobody asks, while the fee and the deadline — the two fields that decide whether you enter — were absent. It is still loaded and still drives the barrel's data; it simply is not card content.

The venue address replaces the country-and-season line, and is copyable

The card used to carry Polska · sezon SPWS-2025-2026. Both halves were already on screen elsewhere: the country is the flag on the city line, and the season is engraved on the barrel seam directly above — the row restated what the surrounding UI had already said. It now holds txt_venue_address with a 22px copy-icon button.

What lands on the clipboard is the full address, not the visible fragment — venue, city and country name joined: Complexe Sportif Omnisports des Vauzelles, Francja, or Pabianice, Polska where there is no venue. The card can afford to show a flag because the reader can see it; a clipboard string pasted into a maps app or sent to a driver cannot. This is also why dropping the country name from display costs nothing: it survives where it is actually needed.

When an event has a city but no venue, the copy button attaches to the city line instead, so the affordance follows whatever location the card has. It is absent only when the event has no location at all. In the mock that is 56 of 103 cards with a copy button and 11 with a full venue row.

The button carries no visible text — an inline SVG glyph only, at 22×22. That does not remove it from the locale files: with no text node, its aria-label and title are its label, so both are translated and both are updated to Skopiowano / Copied on success. An icon-only control with no accessible name is an unlabelled button to a screen reader. Keep the SVG inline rather than reaching for an icon font or sprite sheet — the embed runs under a strict CSP, and that would be one more asset that can fail to load.

Use navigator.clipboard.writeText() with an execCommand fallback: the Clipboard API requires a secure context, and the public spws-calendar embed may sit on a plain-http host page. Confirm the copy visibly — the mock swaps the icon to a checkmark for 1.4s — because a clipboard write is otherwise completely silent.

Entry list — shown until the event starts, not until entries close

DECIDED 9 August: accepted. The entry-list link appears only while dt_start is still in the future, and never on a cancelled event. This is a deliberate change from current behaviour. Today CalendarView.svelte:57 gates it on regOpen, the same deadline ?? dt_start cutoff used for the registration link — so on an event with a stored deadline, the entry list disappears the moment entries close.

That is the wrong rule for this link. Who has entered becomes more interesting once the list is final: you check the draw, you see who is in your category, you decide whether the trip is worth it. Registration and the entry list answer different questions and should not share a cutoff. Consequence for implementation: registrationState() must return the two flags separately rather than one regOpen boolean.

Correction — the test cost stated here was wrong. An earlier draft of this section said CalendarView.test.ts:580 and its neighbours need rewriting to the new rule rather than porting. A read of all 49 tests on 9 August found that no existing test pins the old rule, so nothing needs rewriting:

  • Every test touching .entry-list-link sets dt_registration_deadline: futureStr where futureStr is dt_start, both roughly two months out. Under dt_start > today they all still render the link and still pass.
  • Line 580 asserts absence when url_entry_list is null — a URL check, not a date gate. Unaffected.
  • The only past-deadline test, 8.25, asserts .registration-link and .registration-deadline only, on a 2020-01-01 / COMPLETED event — the entry list is hidden under either rule.

So this change costs new tests, not rewrites, and the §06 test-triage rewrite bucket is empty on this account. The uncomfortable half is the reason it was cheap: the current coupling of the entry list to regOpen was never covered, so nothing would have caught it drifting in either direction. Write the new tests to pin both halves — link present on a future event whose deadline has passed, absent on a cancelled one.

All three keys already exist in both locale files — no new strings are needed for fees, so this is reuse, not translation work. Two rules the current component follows and the card must keep:

  • The tiers are independent, not a set. An event may fill the base fee and _3w while leaving _2w null. Render one line per non-null field — never a block gated on the base fee being present, which would hide a tier-only event's pricing entirely. Asserted at CalendarView.test.ts:770 and :783.
  • A tier can only exist where the tier does. An event covering foil alone has no two- or three-weapon price to quote, so a populated _2w on a single-weapon event is a data error, not something to render. The mock gates the tiers on the event's own weapon count and the reconciliation check proposed for arr_weapons should flag the disagreement rather than the card quietly displaying an impossible price.
  • The currency is stored, not assumed. txt_entry_fee_currency ?? 'PLN' — the fallback is PLN, but PEW events are priced in EUR and GBP, and a hardcoded zł suffix would misprice every foreign competition on the calendar. The same currency applies to all three lines.

Coverage is thin — 28 of 103 events carry a base fee and just 1 carries tiers — so on most cards this whole block is absent. That is correct behaviour, not a bug: it is the strongest case for the omit-when-empty rule, since a card padded with three empty fee rows would bury the fields that are filled.

Dates — the card has room for the range the list never had

dt_end is loaded and used twice today, but never shown: getEventDisplayStatus(enum_status, dt_end, dt_start) derives awaiting-results from it, and the cancelled-notice window counts seven days from it. The list rendered dt_start alone because a 56px date column had no room for more. The card does. Most competitions run a weekend, and 10–11 stycznia answers a question 10 stycznia leaves open — whether to book two nights. Render the range when dt_end differs from dt_start, the single date when it does not. Panels stay single-date; the space argument still holds there.

CountryFlag is blocked until countries are normalised

Phase 3 builds CountryFlag first, but it has nothing to key off. txt_country is free text in mixed languages — Polska, Germany, GRUZJA, and both Great Britain and United Kingdom for one country — and a flag component needs ISO-3166 alpha-2. The mock only draws flags because it carries a hardcoded 21-entry ISO lookup.

Do not let this stall the component. Put the same mapping in a pure, tested countryCode(raw) function inside calendarQuarters.ts, returning null for anything unrecognised so the card renders the country name without a flag rather than the wrong flag. The enrichment pass in §05 then makes that function a near-identity, and its tests become the regression net proving the pass did not change what anyone sees. This is an ordering dependency the phase list does not currently state.

Panel type buckets — the fourth one already exists

The retired strip typed events into four buckets via slotTypeClass(): pew, imew (which absorbs IMEW, IMSW, MEW, MSW, PSW), mpw, and ppw. But look at the stylesheet — .slot.ppw.completed and .slot.mpw.completed share one rule, as do their .planned pair. MPW is classified separately and then painted identically. The distinction was built and never cashed in. The mock goes further and folds MPW into ppw outright, dropping even the unused class.

This matters for open item 4, which asks for championships to be marked distinctly so finished seasons keep a focal point once next-upcoming has nothing to point at. That is not new machinery — it is styling a bucket the code has carried, unstyled, all along. Keep the four-way split in calendarQuarters.ts, and let the barrel decide whether the fourth one gets its own treatment. Deciding it is not a prerequisite for building the barrel; discarding the bucket would make it one later.

Props that die with the old view

isActiveSeason exists only to gate the rolling strip — CalendarView.svelte:34 and :269 are its sole readers. When the strip goes it becomes a lie: a prop that is threaded through, type-checked, and consulted by nothing. Delete it from the props interface, from the call site at App.svelte:81, and delete the derivation at App.svelte:399, which is computed for no other consumer. Same for onseasonchange and the meaning of selectedSeasonId once the barrel owns season state — already noted in Phase 4, but isActiveSeason was not.

The PPW | +EVF control and the season field that rules it

The scope control stays, and it is not unconditional. It is governed per season by the scoring-config field show_evf_toggle_calendar, shown to admins as “Pokaż przełącznik +EVF w Kalendarzu” (sc_show_evf_toggle_calendar, edited at SeasonManager.svelte:54). It reaches the view like this:

StepWhere

Four things to carry across exactly:

  • It is per-season scoring config, not a column on tbl_season, and it is fetched through an RPC rather than selected from a view. Do not go looking for a boolean column to join.
  • The default is true — an unset season shows the control. This is the opposite of its ranklist sibling show_evf_toggle, which defaults to false. ADR-044 amend split the two deliberately so the surfaces are independent; do not re-merge them.
  • Off does not merely hide the button — it constrains the data. CalendarView.svelte:291 reads !showEvfToggle || scopeFilter === 'ppw', so with the config off the calendar is domestic-only regardless of scope state. Hiding the control without forcing the scope would strand EVF events on screen with no way to filter them. In the mock this is the difference between 103 panels and 22.
  • It loads asynchronously, which is the whole reason for the $effect at CalendarView.svelte:225-227: the scope default re-syncs from showEvfToggle on every change until the user picks explicitly (scopeUserOverride). Port that effect with the state into the orchestrator — a scope initialised once at mount will be wrong for the first paint.

The mock carries a review-only checkbox above the phone frame, outside the device, so both states can be seen. It is not part of the UI — the real switch lives in the season admin.

// state — stays in CalendarView, which is why CalendarFilters.svelte is not a file
let scopeFilter: 'all' | 'ppw' = $state('ppw')
let scopeUserOverride = $state(false)

// showEvfToggle arrives async from the season scoring config, so the default
// re-syncs on every change until the user picks a scope explicitly.
$effect(() => {
  if (!scopeUserOverride) scopeFilter = showEvfToggle ? 'all' : 'ppw'
})

// markup — only rendered when the season config allows it
{#if showEvfToggle}
  <div class="scope-filters">
    <button class:active={scopeFilter === 'ppw'}
      onclick={() => { scopeFilter = 'ppw'; scopeUserOverride = true }}>PPW</button>
    <button class:active={scopeFilter === 'all'}
      onclick={() => { scopeFilter = 'all'; scopeUserOverride = true }}>+EVF</button>
  </div>
{/if}

// and the data rule that must travel with it — hiding the control is not enough
if (!showEvfToggle || scopeFilter === 'ppw') {
  result = result.filter((e) => !isInternationalEvent(e))
}

isInternationalEvent is bool_has_international || /^(PEW|MEW|MSW|PSW|IMEW|IMSW)/.test(txt_code) — port it into calendarQuarters.ts so the filter is unit-testable without mounting anything.

Localisation — and the Polish plural trap

Every string in the barrel and the card goes through t() and locales/{en,pl}.json. The mock now demonstrates the full EN | PL switch working across months (short and full), weapon names, country names, event names, the four status labels, the awaiting and cancelled sentences, the results and invitation links, the empty-quarter label, and the view title. Registry codes (SPWS, EVF, FIE), event codes and device names are proper nouns and stay untranslated.

Two things a naive port gets wrong:

  • Polish months in a date are genitive. 18 kwietnia, not 18 kwiecień. Because they only ever appear inside a date here, the genitive forms can be stored directly — but a month_N key reused for a standalone month heading elsewhere would then read wrongly, so the calendar needs its own keys rather than borrowing the existing month_1…12.
  • Polish has three plural forms, and the count picks between them. 1 turniej · 2–4 turnieje · 0 and 5+ turniejów — with the trap that 12, 13 and 14 take the genitive despite ending in 2–4, while 22, 23 and 24 do not. English needs only a singular/plural pair, so the template count + ' ' + noun looks right in EN and is wrong for roughly half of all counts in PL. The current calendar has this defect live in both languages: tournaments_count is a flat string — "turniejów" in pl.json:90, "tournaments" in en.json:90 — concatenated with the count at CalendarView.svelte:72. So 2 turniejów and 1 tournaments are both on screen today, and events with a single tournament are common. This needs a pluralisation helper keyed by count, not a flat string; the mock carries a reference implementation for both languages.

Reuse rather than reinvent

  • getEventDisplayStatus() in lib/eventStatus.ts — already exhaustive over all eight EventStatus values and already handles awaiting-results. EventCard consumes it unchanged.
  • fetchPriorSeasonEvents(seasonIds) at lib/api.ts:206 — already queries vw_calendar across multiple seasons. The barrel's multi-season need is a caller change, not a new query. But note the ordering: this one sorts .order('txt_code') while fetchCalendarEvents at api.ts:172 sorts .order('dt_start'). Since txt_code orders PEW10 before PEW2, the multi-season path arrives in an order that is neither chronological nor numeric. calendarQuarters.ts must sort by dt_start itself and never inherit caller order — and a test should feed it deliberately shuffled input so that requirement is pinned rather than accidentally satisfied.
  • t() from lib/locale.svelte.ts with locales/{en,pl}.json for every new string.
  • The CREATED-hiding and cancelled-notice-window rules already in CalendarView.svelte — port them into calendarQuarters.ts unchanged. They are ADR-037 behaviour, not incidental.

05Data changes

Multi-season load

fetchCalendarEvents(seasonId) at api.ts:172 is single-season. Add fetchCalendarEventsAllSeasons() alongside it, or call fetchPriorSeasonEvents with every season id. Volume is modest — roughly 20 events per season — so a single up-front load is simpler than lazy-loading on boundary approach, and it removes the fetch-per-rotation problem entirely.

09Open items

Raised during design and recorded here rather than guessed at.

  1. Card opening state — still open. Next upcoming, most recent result, or time-sensitive. Recommendation: time-sensitive. The Monday after a competition weekend everyone wants results; three weeks later nobody does. The seven-day window already exists in the codebase for cancelled-event notices, so it is not a new concept to explain. Do not read the mock as having settled this — it opens on next-upcoming because that was the simplest thing to demonstrate, not because it was chosen. This and item 4 are the only two open items that reach into Phase 1, and both land in resolveAnchorQuarter(), which is why that function is written last.
  2. Scroll behaviour — barrel pinned versus whole page scrolling. Recommendation: scrolling. Pinning ~250px of drum above every card on a 568px screen leaves under half the display for the thing you came to read, and the barrel is not used continuously — you spin it, pick, then read.
  3. Weapon preference lifetime — moot, closed. This asked whether the calendar's weapon filter should persist in localStorage and whether it should share state with the ranking tab's BROŃ selector. There is no weapon filter, so there is no preference to persist. Left visible rather than deleted so it is not re-raised as an oversight.
  4. Past-season anchor — finished seasons have no next-upcoming event, so the drum loses its focal point and rows go flat. Recommendation: mark championship events (MPW, MŚW, MEW) distinctly so past rows keep structure without needing viewer identity, which the public embed cannot have.
