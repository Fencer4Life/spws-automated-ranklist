A scoring engine under which every fencer gains from every join

Today's engine and every variant of §8 we discussed make joining cost points for between a fifth and a half of the fencers involved. A different construction, built only from quantities that a joiner cannot take away, makes every join a gain for everyone. It keeps ordinary brackets within about five points of today.

0 · The basics, on a 10-fencer bracket

A fencer's points are the sum of three parts. Each part does one job, and none of them can go down when another category joins the bracket.

PartWhat it paysWho gets itIts job
1 · Field2 × log₂NEvery fencer in the bracket, including the lastA bigger bracket is worth more to everyone in it.
2 · Fencers beaten3 points for each fencer who finished below you on the piste, of any category (counted up to 31)Everyone except the lastThe place on the piste counts. Every place higher is worth 3 more.
3 · Medal in your categorygold 27 × ∛N, silver 15 × ∛N, bronze 6 × ∛NThe first three of each category in the bracketWinning your category is the big reward, and it grows with the size of the bracket.
Your question: which medals get a bonus? All three — gold, silver and bronze — in every category of the bracket. The bonus follows the medal handed out at the ceremony. A bracket joining three categories can therefore pay up to nine medal bonuses, three per category. What decides the medal is your place among your own category. What decides its size is N, the whole bracket.
How "58 / 32 / 13 at N = 10" is calculated The medal bonus is a fixed weight multiplied by the cube root of N, where N is the size of the whole joined bracket, not of your category.
gold = 27 × ∛N at N = 10: 27 × 2.154 = 58.17 silver = 15 × ∛N 15 × 2.154 = 32.32 bronze = 6 × ∛N 6 × 2.154 = 12.93
The cube root grows slowly: doubling the bracket raises every medal by 26%. Gold is worth 42.9 at N = 4, 54.0 at N = 8, 68.0 at N = 16 and 85.7 at N = 32. Today's podium bonus uses the same cube-root curve (3 × ∛N times 3 / 2 / 1). Only the weights are larger here, for the reason given below the next table.
Must someone be behind you to get the medal bonus? Yes — but under the recommended rule (E2), anyone on the piste, not necessarily someone from your own category. Your medal is decided inside your category. The bonus is paid only if at least one fencer of any category finished below you. Four cases in the 10-fencer bracket:
  • V1 silver of 2, finished 4th: beat 6 fencers → silver bonus 32.32. Under the §6 rule (a category-mate behind you) he would get nothing, because the other V1 finished ahead.
  • V1 silver of 2, finished last (10th): beat nobody → no bonus under either rule.
  • A lone V4, the only one in the bracket, finished 9th: gold of a one-fencer category, beat 1 fencer → gold bonus 58.17, 67.81 in total. Under the §6 rule: nothing, since he has no category-mate at all.
  • The same lone V4, finished last: beat nobody → no bonus, 6.64 in total.
The third case is the most generous one the rule allows. A lone older fencer who comes, joins and beats a single fencer collects a full gold bonus. That is the strongest possible pull to join for exactly the categories that fence alone today. But it pays more than a 3rd place on the piste in another category (the V2 silver, 59.96). If that is too generous, one variant keeps the incentive while shrinking the lone-fencer case: scale the medal by the category's size as well. That is the "moderate" variant in §9 item 5.

Why 27 / 15 / 6 — and what half of that would do

The weights are a calibration, not a principle. The property "nobody loses by joining" holds for any positive weights. 27 / 15 / 6 are what makes an ordinary bracket score most like today's engine (least squares over the 1,550 real results in brackets of 4 or more, then rounded).

They are that large because today's engine is steep at the top. At N = 8 the gap from 1st to 4th is 57 points, while the gap from 4th to 8th is only 20. The "fencers beaten" part adds the same 3 points for every place, so it can supply only 9 of those 57. The medal has to supply the rest. The weights decide one policy question: how much is winning your category worth compared with each place on the piste? At 27 × ∛8 = 54, an N = 8 gold is worth as much as 18 places. Halved, it is worth 9.

Limiting the inflation — a better way than halving (decision E3)

Some inflation cannot be avoided. If every fencer gains when brackets join, the total must rise when brackets join. The pull to join is the extra points. What can be chosen is how large the extra is and where it comes from: earned (by beating more fencers) or given (because the bracket grew).

In the report's version, half of the extra comes from the medal growing with the joined bracket. A category's gold is worth more because other categories turned up. That part is given, not earned, and it is also what makes the lone-fencer case so generous. Halving the weights shrinks it but keeps it growing with other categories. The better fix is to size the medal by the fencer's own category: a gold won against three category-mates is worth the same whether or not other categories joined. Joining then pays mostly through beating more fencers.

How to read it

  • Everyone gains, from +2.64 for the last to +89.81 for the winner. Under today's engine with §8, the V3 gold and silver lose 33.59 and 14.03 by joining.
  • Place counts. The V2 gold (2nd on the piste) earns 88.81 and the V3 gold (5th) earns 79.81. The 9-point gap is 3 places × 3 points.
  • The winner's total is close to today's. V1-a earns 91.81 (6.64 + 27 + 58.17). Today the winner of 10 earns 92.61 (33.2 place + 40 DE + 19.4 podium). The total is about the same; only its make-up differs.

1 · The question, and a correction

The question. How should the engine change so that every fencer gains when brackets are joined, and fencers are motivated to ask for more joining every time?

The correction. In round 4 I wrote that "points follow the medals". That was wrong. In a joined bracket, the medals of each category go to that category's highest finishers, but points must still reflect the place on the piste. A category gold won in 3rd place overall must earn more than a category gold won in 7th or 12th place. This analysis keeps both: a medal bonus for the category medal, and points that depend on the place.

Two features of this table matter here:

  • Only the top benefits from a bigger field. The winner earns 54 → 78 → 103 → 129 as N goes 4 → 8 → 16 → 32. The middle finisher earns about 21 at every size from 8 to 48, and last place always earns 1.

4 · Why today's engine cannot make everyone gain

When a fencer from another category joins and finishes ahead of you, your overall place drops by one while N rises by one. Every term of today's engine that depends on overall place — the ln p curve, the DE round thresholds, the overall podium — can then fall, and the small increase in N does not make up for it. For a winner overtaken by a stronger joiner, the whole winner's premium is lost.

That holds for every variant we discussed, including my round-4 idea. The check covers every joined size N ≤ 40, every category size, every category place and every possible overall place: 123,410 configurations.

The general rule behind this Any bonus tied to a threshold of overall position — the podium places 1–3, "last 8" and other DE rounds, or a steep curve on the place itself — is taken away by a stronger joiner. No tuning of today's formula fixes this. A top-heavy curve on overall place and "every join is a gain" cannot both hold.

5 · The principle: score only what a joiner cannot take away

When one more fencer joins the bracket, three quantities can only stay the same or grow, wherever the joiner finishes:

QuantityJoiner finishes ahead of youJoiner finishes behind you
N — the size of the joined bracket+1+1
b — fencers who finished behind you (b = N − p)unchanged+1
m — your place within your own categoryunchanged (the joiner is from another category)unchanged
Consequence If points are a sum of one term growing with N, one growing with b, and a medal bonus fixed by m and growing with N, then no join can lower anyone's points. As long as the N term grows strictly, every join raises everyone's points. That is exactly the property you asked for, and it holds for any finishing order, not just typical ones. Place still matters: within one bracket, a better place means a larger b.

6 · The proposed engine

This is the additive form fitted to today's engine on the 1,550 real results in brackets of 4 or more, then rounded to numbers that are easy to state.

field = 2 · log₂N every fencer in the bracket beaten = 3 · min(b, 31) 3 points per fencer who finished behind you medal = (27, 15, 6) · ∛N your gold / silver / bronze in YOUR category, only if a category-mate finished behind you (E2 in §0 recommends: if you beat anyone on the piste) points = (field + beaten + medal) · type multiplier N < 4 → flat table: N − p + 1 (decision R1, domestic types only)

N and p are those of the bracket as fenced (joined N, place on the piste). m is the place among the fencer's own category. In an ordinary one-category bracket m = p, and the formula is just field + beaten + podium.

Draft wording for the regulation (Polish) Punkty za wynik to suma trzech składników: premii za liczebność turnieju (2 × log₂ liczby zawodników), 3 punktów za każdego zawodnika sklasyfikowanego niżej (najwyżej 31) oraz premii medalowej za miejsca 1–3 w swojej kategorii wiekowej, przyznawanej, jeżeli w tej kategorii ktoś został sklasyfikowany niżej. W turnieju połączonym liczebność oznacza liczbę wszystkich zawodników turnieju.

  • Y, the silver medallist, earns no medal bonus. She finished behind her only category-mate, so the guard in §6 applies. Joining still pays her +4.61.

9 · Costs and risks

1 · Points grow with joining, by design Measured against the three seasons as fenced under today's engine without the table, total points are −14% with no joining (the table cuts small brackets), +56% with J8 and +156% with every category joined. With the medal tied to the category's own size instead: +39% and +124%. Each ranking compares fencers of one category only, so within a season the order is decided by who joins and wins, not by the scale. The scale matters in three places: the combined Kadra ranking (item 2), comparisons with carried-over results from 2025/26, and any published "points" figure people compare across seasons.
2 · EVF results must stay on today's engine One engine scores every tournament type in a season. Applied to EVF results (median N 8, often 20–40), the proposed engine raises their mean by 32% (25% with the cap), against +4–5% for domestic results as fenced. That would shift the combined national-team ranking toward EVF results. The proposed engine should apply to PPW and MPW only, with international types kept on SPWS_FIELD_SCALED_V1_2026_2027. The same goes for the flat table: several MEW and MSW brackets store a small N (median 3), and the table would cut them to 1–3 points.
3 · DE rounds stop being scored as steps Today a fencer who reaches the last 8 earns a DE round bonus that a 9th place does not. The proposed engine replaces those steps with 3 points per fencer beaten. This is not optional: any step tied to overall position is lost to a stronger joiner (§4).
4 · Continuity with the seasons as fenced Re-scoring 2023/24–2025/26 unchanged (no joining) with the proposed engine instead of today's engine + table, using best 4 PPW + MPW: 85 sub-rankings, mean Spearman rank correlation 0.981 (lowest 0.83), 1 change of leader, 2 changes to a top-3, and 215 of 656 fencers move at least one position. Ordinary brackets stay close to today (RMSE 5.0 points on 1,550 results).
5 · Medal scaled by joined N or by the category's own size Scaling the medal by the joined N gives the strongest pull: a V4 gold of 2 earns 53.8 at N = 5 and 91.2 at N = 25. Scaling by the category's own size keeps the medal proportional to the contest within the category (41.7 → 46.3 over the same range), with less inflation. Both variants leave nobody worse off. Only the first makes a bigger join clearly better than a small one for the medallists of small categories, which is the goal you set.
6 · The medal guard Without any condition, a lone V4 in a joined bracket of 25 would collect a medal bonus of about 79 points even after beating nobody. The condition in §6 (a category-mate behind you) blocks that, but it also denies the bonus to a silver medallist of a two-fencer category who beat six others. Decision E2 in §0 replaces it with "you beat at least one fencer on the piste". All three variants keep the property.

11 · What implementing it involves

  • A new released engine version (working name SPWS_FIELD_BEATEN_MEDAL_V1_2026_2027). Its function needs the category place m and the category size k as well as N and p. fn_calc_tournament_scores derives both for a joined slice: m is the rank of the row's place inside its own tournament and k is the tournament's row count. For every other tournament m = p and k = N.
  • Engine per tournament type. Today one engine per season scores every type. This needs either an engine per type in tbl_scoring_type_config, or an engine version that keeps the V1 formula for international types. That is an ADR-level choice.
  • §8 whole-bracket storage from the joined-category plan: every sibling stores the joined N, and each row keeps its place on the piste. Without it, the engine never sees a joined bracket.
  • Governance. 2026/27 is unlocked, so the switch is an ordinary pre-lock change, provided it lands before PPW1 is scored. The public calculator and Załącznik nr 1 follow the engine automatically (ADR-102) and would need the category inputs for joined brackets.
  • Board adoption of the three-part rule and the flat table, replacing the current point table in the regulation.

12 · Method and reproducibility

  • Data. LOCAL domestic results of the three seasons, verified identical in bracket and result counts to the PROD figures of the 22 September small-bracket report. The PROD lock state was read with scripts/cloud-sql.sh prod (read-only, 27 September).
  • Engine port. A Python port of fn_score_spws_field_scaled_v1_2026_2027, checked against the database function (for example 7 fencers: 75.29 / 49.91 / 28.53 / 18.79 / 5.68 / 3.14 / 1.00).
  • Fit. Least squares against today's engine over the 1,550 real results in brackets of 4 or more, by grid search followed by random refinement, then rounded to 2 / 3 / (27, 15, 6).
  • Replays. Categories merged as described in §7. Each category's internal order is always preserved; the interleaving between categories is the unknown that the orders and random merges cover.
  • Ranking comparison. Best 4 PPW + MPW (×1.2) per season × weapon × gender × category, the rule in force for 2024/25 and 2025/26.
  • Scripts. The analysis scripts are in this session's scratchpad (engine.py, props.py, sim.py, report_numbers.py, examples.py). If this goes to the board, they should be committed as a re-runnable tool, the same way the small-bracket report generator was.
