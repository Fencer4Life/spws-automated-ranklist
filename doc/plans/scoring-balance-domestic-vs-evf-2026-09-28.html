Why a domestic podium earns half an EVF one, and what the medals can do

For places 1–4 in the brackets where PPW is usually fenced, EVF classic gives 1.5 to 2.5 times what the 2026/27 SPWS engine gives. Raising the medals to 36 / 21 / 9 × ∛K, plus 5 × ∛K for fourth place, closes most of that gap. It leaves every place below fourth exactly as the board approved it.

Short answer

You are right for small and middle brackets. The gap depends on N

  • N = 4–10: EVF gives 1.5–2.5× for places 1–4. That is where PPW brackets with medals usually sit.
  • N = 12–16: the gap narrows to 1.0–1.5×.
  • N ≈ 20–24: the two engines meet.
  • N = 28–31: SPWS gives more than EVF. At 32 the engine switches to EVF, and the winner drops from 150.8 to 128.6.
  • N ≤ 3: the table gives 3 / 2 / 1, while EVF gives 83 / 38 / 5. No medal constant changes this range, and it holds 156 of the 558 domestic results.

Why the shapes differ

EVF pays for reaching the podium: the winner starts from 50, earns 10 per elimination round won and a podium bonus, whatever the field. SPWS pays for the fencers you finish above: 3.5 each, plus a medal that grows slowly (∛K). A small field therefore pays little under SPWS and a lot under EVF. A big field is the other way round, because 3.5 per fencer grows in a straight line while EVF's place scale is logarithmic.

Where the fencers actually are

Most domestic brackets are small; PEW categories run larger

  • 161 of 293 domestic category brackets have N ≤ 3, scored by the table, where medals do not apply.
  • From 2026/27 a joined PPW bracket is scored whole: N is the physical bracket, K the fencer’s category. Where categories are fenced together, N grows toward the per-event totals seen in the data (10–56), and SPWS points grow with it.
  • A PEW category is scored at its own N. A PEW win in a category of 6 earns 96; a PPW win in a category of 6 earns 43.7.

C · Type coefficient: PPW and MPW × 1.74

This is a configuration change only, with no new engine: 1.74 is the weighted EVF / SPWS ratio for places 1–4. It lifts every domestic place, including the lower ones that are already level with EVF (the ratio for all places is 1.35), and it leaves the N-dependence unchanged.

D · One yardstick: score international results with the SPWS engine too

This removes the root cause: EVF over-rewards small international categories exactly as it would domestic ones. Domestic and international then differ only by coefficient (MEW 1.3, MSW 1.4). But international totals fall to about 43% of today’s. It reverses the ADR-103 decision that kept those types on EVF classic, and results carried over from 2025/26 keep their EVF points for this season.

E · Count fewer international results (best 3 instead of 5)

On its own this is a small lever: 76% → 72.5% for fencers who travel. Combined with A+ it reaches 59%.

Constraints

What any change costs, and when

  • Timing. The first scored 2026/27 result (PPW1, expected around 29 September) locks the season’s scoring (ADR-097). A coefficient change (C) is then only possible through the audited revision. The engine constants in A, A+ and B belong to the engine itself, not the season, so after results are scored they would need a rescore.
  • The approved table. 13 / 7 / 3 and 3.5 are in the board-approved points table and the published Załącznik nr 1. A, A+ and B change them, so they need the board’s approval of a revised table. Because no result has been scored on the engine yet, the constants can be amended in place rather than as a second engine version.
  • The code. The constants live in two places, the SQL strategy and frontend/src/lib/scoring.ts, which the calculator and the annex are generated from; one pgTAP and one Vitest parity suite pin them. Adding a fourth-place medal is a small change to the same formula.
  • Carried results. Until each 2026/27 PPW is fenced, 2025/26 PPW results scored by EVF classic stay in the domestic column (for example 109.4 for last season’s PPW4 win at N = 10). Today a new PPW win at N = 8 earns 53.5, half of that; under A+ it earns 99.5.
  • Data caveat. MEW categories are stored with a median N of 1, too small to be real fields, so MEW’s share here is understated.
