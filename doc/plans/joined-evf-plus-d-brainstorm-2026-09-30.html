EVF + d: one short rule for every joined bracket

You asked why clause 3d is needed, and whether 3, 4 and 5 joined categories can have something simple. Your idea, EVF + d with a cap, keeps every principle for small categories, holds premiums to 4 points or less, and needs no chains. It works for pairs too, so it can replace clauses 3a–3d entirely. One question at the end, about the small category whose only neighbour has 4+ fencers.

1 · Why clause 3d was there

Short answer: only because of the exception

3d applies only when a category of 4+ is an older category in a joined bracket. The joining rules allow that in one case: a small category at the young end whose only neighbour has 4+ fencers, for example V0 3 next to V1 7. Without joining, that V0 would score by the table. It happened in 25 brackets in three seasons.

You are right that it gains little and complicates a lot: a floor, then raises up the list. If a category of 4+ never joins as an older category, 3d is never needed. That brings back the small category with nobody to join, which is the question in §6.

2 · Your idea, written as a rule

Reguła punktacji (propozycja)
  1. Stawka 1–3 zawodników: N − p + 1 pkt, czyli 3, 2, 1.
  2. Stawka jednej kategorii wiekowej albo stawka 16 i więcej zawodników: punkty EVF za miejsce w stawce.
  3. Stawka łączona 4–15 zawodników: zawodnik dostaje punkty EVF za miejsce w całej stawce plus d pkt, gdzie d to różnica między jego kategorią a najmłodszą kategorią w stawce. Przykład: w stawce z V0 zawodnik V1 ma d = 1, a zawodnik V4 ma d = 4; najmłodsza kategoria ma d = 0.

    Nikt nie dostaje więcej niż zawodnik, który zajął miejsce przed nim, minus 1 pkt.

  4. Wynik mnoży się przez współczynnik rangi zawodów (PPW 1,0; MPW 1,2).

Każde miejsce jest rozstrzygnięte walką, więc w stawce nie ma miejsc ex aequo.

How I read "capping". The cap is only an upper limit: it lowers a score to 1 point under the fencer placed ahead, and never lifts anyone. The lift of clause 3b ("miejsce wyżej − 1") goes away.

If you meant to keep the lift and only change "+ 1" to "+ d", the lift would still need this cap, because EVF + 4 can reach the fencer ahead. That version is longer, not shorter.

Why it works
  • Small categories gain anyway. A category of 1–3 alone scores 3 / 2 / 1. The joined bracket pays EVF, which is at least that for every place, and + d makes it strictly more. The premium does not have to carry the incentive.
  • The cap never reaches the youngest and never goes below EVF. For 4–15 fencers, neighbouring EVF places are at least 1.25 points apart. So a cap can only trim part of a fencer's + d.
  • No chains. Each score needs only its own place, its d, and the score of the fencer one place ahead.
  • The cap is rare. It binds in 1.9% of older results in the real brackets, mostly near the bottom, where EVF places are close together.

The youngest category of 4+ still pays, as before: below its own bracket in 40.3% of its results, when older fencers place ahead of it. EVF + d does not change that, because the youngest category's score is plain EVF under both rules.

Correction to the previous page

The place-ahead rule does not always keep Z4. When an older fencer takes "miejsce wyżej − 1" and a teammate directly behind takes "EVF + 1", the gap between them can fall below 1 point at the bottom of a large bracket.

  • It happens in the real data in V2 12 + V3 2 brackets: GP7-2023-2024 Sabre M, and PPW3-2025-2026 Sabre M under rules 1–5. When the two V3 fencers finish 13th and 14th, there are 0.86 points between them.
  • Across all pairs up to 15 fencers, the gap goes down to 0.59.

The previous page reported "smallest gap 1.00" because my 3d code raised more fencers than the written clause says. EVF + d cannot do this: its cap is Z4 itself.

What you give up: the big lift for the older fencer directly behind a youngest fencer. Example 4: 66.67 instead of 97.00. The older fencer still gains far more than their own bracket would pay: 2.00 there.

6 · Question

A small category at the young end whose only neighbour has 4+ fencers: join or not?

You wrote that a category of 4+ goes on its own. For most brackets that is already the rule. The exception is a small V0 (sometimes V1) at the young end: its only neighbour has 4+ fencers, and there is nobody younger to join.

  1. Join anyway, with the same EVF + d rule and no 3drecommended. The table stays unused. The category of 4+ pays: below its own bracket in 38.8% of its results in these 24 brackets. A youngest category of 4+ pays 40.3% everywhere else, so every category of 4+ is treated alike, whether it is the youngest or not. Z2 then reads:

    „Z2. Kategoria, która ma 1–3 zawodników, zawsze zyskuje na łączeniu. Zapłacić może tylko kategoria, która ma co najmniej 4 zawodników i mogłaby startować osobno.”

  2. The category of 4+ always fences alone. The small category scores by the 3 / 2 / 1 table: 21 small categories and 45 fencers in three seasons, 18 of them V0.

Why the recommendation: it keeps both of your goals, "never the table" and "simple". It adds no clause, and the only fencers who can lose points are in categories big enough to fence alone.
