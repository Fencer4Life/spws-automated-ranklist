Joined age categories: §8 plus a flat table for small brackets

If brackets under four fencers are worth only 1–3 points, joining becomes the better choice for the older category in almost every case. The younger category then loses points only to an older fencer who actually beats them.

Round 5 — see the engine analysis joined-bracket-scoring-engine-analysis-2026-09-27.html shows that §8 with the table (R1) and with the category medal at N ≥ 8 (R2) still leaves 9–37% of fencers worse off when categories join. It proposes an engine under which every fencer gains from every join (decision E1). R2 is superseded by it.

1 · Why this is being done

8 — Wyniki w połączonych kategoriach wiekowych. 1. W przypadku zawodów rozgrywanych w połączonych kategoriach wiekowych wyniki przypisuje się zgodnie z zajętym miejscem, bez dodatkowego rozdzielania kategorii w celu obliczenia punktów rankingowych.

The goal is for older categories to be willing to join younger ones instead of fencing tiny brackets of their own. Round 2 (§7) showed that §8 alone works against that on the 2026/2027 engine: a small bracket still pays more than a low place in a joined one.

2 · Your proposal — a flat table for N < 4

For brackets of 1, 2 or 3 fencers, points = N − place + 1, with no DE or podium bonus. From N = 4 upward the engine is unchanged.

Worst case: every younger fencer finishes ahead

A fencer who is m-th of k in their category has k − m category-mates behind them, so in a joined bracket of N they finish no lower than place N − k + m. Gold of 3 therefore finishes 5th of 7 at worst, never 7th.

In a joined bracket of 7, no older fencer can lose points by joining, even in the worst case. Checked for every joined size from 4 to 64: joining never costs anything up to N = 14. From N = 15 or 16 upward, a gold or silver who finishes behind every younger fencer can lose a fraction of a point, because bottom places on a large field earn barely more than 1. The largest loss is −0.55 for gold of 2 at N = 32 and −1.09 for gold of 3 at N = 32; the extreme is −1.63 at N = 64. Veteran joined brackets are typically well below 15.

A bigger joined field raises every younger fencer's points. A younger fencer loses only when an older fencer finishes ahead of them. That is the sporting result, not a side effect of the rule.

Conclusion §8 plus the flat table gives the incentive you want. The older category never loses by joining a bracket of up to 14 fencers, the younger one gains unless it is beaten, and rule D from round 2 becomes unnecessary. The table must never be combined with the split: that would cut small categories to 1–3 points and leave them no way to recover by joining (split keeps them at 2 and 1).

4 · Side effects to weigh

1 · Fencers pay for the organizer's decision Joining is decided on the day, not by the fencer. If an organizer runs a small category separately, its fencers get 1–3 points through no choice of their own. On PROD in 2025/2026, 86 of 145 domestic brackets (59%) had N < 4. They held 28% of results and 32.5% of raw points (small-bracket report, 22 September). The table only works together with a regulation that obliges the organizer to join a category below 4 with an adjacent one.
2 · A cliff at N = 4 1st of 3 earns 3 points and 1st of 4 earns 54.29. A fourth entrant is worth 51 points to the winner, which rewards padding a bracket. N already counts only fencers with a result, so a no-show does not count, but a fencer who withdraws after one bout does.
3 · Details to settle later Do type multipliers apply to the table (MPW ×1.2 would make 1st of 2 worth 2.4)? How do ties work (places 1, 2, 2 → 3, 2, 2 points under N − place + 1)? And the joined bracket itself can be under 4 (V3 1 + V4 2 = N 3), in which case it takes the table too.

6 · Going further — making N ≥ 8 attractive

The question. The flat table makes joining better than staying apart. It does not make a bigger join better than a small one. What would make an older category want to join two younger ones so the bracket reaches 8 or more?

Under §8 alone, a weaker fencer prefers the smallest join

A V3 pair (gold and silver of 2) that finishes at the bottom of whichever bracket it joins:

The bigger the join, the less the older gold earns. The engine's table in §7 shows why: points for bottom places stay under about 5 at any N (8th of 9 earns 2.6 place points; 10th of 12 earns 3.6). The two levers that grow with N — the base and the podium bonus — only reach the top of the bracket, and that is where the younger fencers finish.

Three levers, and who each one reaches

LeverWhat it doesReaches the older category?
Steeper base (cap reached at N = 16 instead of 32)Raises the winner's points in bigger brackets.Barely. Bottom places stay near 1–5. It pays the younger winners.
Multiplier for joined brackets of N ≥ 8 (for example ×1.2)Raises everyone's points in a qualifying bracket.Proportionally. 12.65 becomes 15.2, 1 becomes 1.2. Most of the extra goes to the top.
Category medal bonus in joined brackets of N ≥ 8The podium bonus is paid for the medal won in your own category, scored on the joined N, instead of for the overall top 3.Yes, directly. It pays exactly the medallists of the small categories, and it grows with the joined N.

Why this lever fits
  • Points follow the medals actually handed out. This is the gold and silver from your first objection.
  • Nobody loses. Category place is never worse than overall place, so every fencer's podium bonus stays the same or rises. The younger category's own medallists gain too, so they have no reason to refuse a join.
  • It stays within its own ranking. Rankings compare only fencers of the same category. A V3 medal from a joined 9 competes only with other V3 results, and a genuine V3 bracket of 4 still pays more (1st of 4 = 54.29) than being carried by a large joined field (31.37).
  • It restores what a small-category gold used to be worth, but only through joining. 1st of 2 earned 31.34 on the current engine. Under the table, staying apart earns 2, and joining 8+ earns about 31.
  • No new columns. Under §8 storage each V-cat tournament holds exactly one category's fencers, so the category place is the rank of the row's place inside its own tournament, and the joined N is its stored N. It goes into the same new engine version as the flat table.
Points to settle
  • The cliff at 8 is intentional. At 7th of 7 a V3 gold earns 1.00; at 8th of 8 with one more joiner, 19.0. That rewards bringing in another category, which is the goal. The risk is padding the bracket. N counts only fencers with a result.
  • A medal with no category opponent. Should a gold of 1 (nobody beaten in the category) get the bonus? I recommend requiring at least one category-mate behind you. Otherwise a lone V4 earns about 18 points without winning a bout against anyone in the category.
  • Domestic slices only. For EVF tournaments only Polish rows are stored, so "rank inside the tournament" would hand podium bonuses to the best Pole. The rule must apply only to domestic joined slices (bool_joint_pool_split, PPW/MPW).
  • 8 or 9? The engine's DE bonus already steps at 9: from 9 fencers the tableau is 16, and places 5–8 earn +10. A threshold of 8 is a full tableau of 8. Both work, and 8 is your number.

9 · Design points that survive any R1 answer

Library and dropdown Each joined-category approach is a registered entry (code, Polish label, rule text), mirroring tbl_scoring_engine, with one FK per season. It appears as a dropdown next to the engine selector in the Season scoring editor and is read-only once locked. The split stays in the library for earlier seasons and future use. Naming (D1) follows once R1 is settled.
Lock The approach is governed by the ADR-097 lock in the four places listed for enum_default_ranking_mode. fn_revise_and_rescore_season refuses to change it, because split rows have lost their joined places.
Storage and scope Every fencer stays in their own V-cat tournament. Under §8 a row keeps its joined place, and every sibling stores the joined N. SPWS-organized events only; EVF and FIE paths are untouched.
