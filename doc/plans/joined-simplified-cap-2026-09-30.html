Research · 30 September 2026 · one joining exception and clause 3d · the 3/2/1 table only where nobody can be joined · checked on three seasons

Place-ahead scoring with one joining exception: the 3/2/1 table is left only where a whole weapon has fewer than 4 fencers

Your decisions of 30 September are in: no +1 for last place, and no ties, because every place is fenced off. This version adds one exception to joining rule 5 and one scoring clause, 3d. With them, no small category fences alone, and older fencers still always gain. On all PPW and MPW of 2023/24–2025/26, the table is then used only in 20 women's foil and sabre events where the whole weapon had 1–3 fencers. One question at the end: what to do with those 20.

What changed since the last version
  • +1 for last place: not added, as you decided.
  • Ties: the tie clause is gone. Every place is fenced off, so the fencer one place ahead is always one person. The rule now says so.
  • Joining: one exception to rule 5 (§2). The 21 small categories that used to fence alone now join. Every joined bracket reaches 4 fencers unless the whole weapon and gender has fewer than 4.
  • Scoring: clause 3d (§1) keeps "older fencers always gain" in the brackets the exception forms.
  • Correction: for brackets of more than 9 fencers, the previous version meant to sample 20,000 finishing orders but scored one order 20,000 times. Every figure here enumerates every distinct order instead, at most 37,800 per bracket. The previous page said a youngest category of 4+ ends below its own bracket in 31% of its results; the correct figure was 40.3%. The other figures moved by 0.1 or not at all.

1 · Reguła punktacji

Reguła punktacji

Stawka to wszyscy zawodnicy jednej listy wyników. N to liczba zawodników w stawce, a p to miejsce w stawce. Każde miejsce jest rozstrzygnięte walką, więc w stawce nie ma miejsc ex aequo.

  1. Stawka 1–3 zawodników: N − p + 1 pkt, czyli 3, 2, 1.
  2. Stawka jednej kategorii wiekowej albo stawka 16 i więcej zawodników: punkty EVF za miejsce w stawce.
  3. Stawka łączona 4–15 zawodników:
    1. zawodnik najmłodszej kategorii w stawce dostaje punkty EVF za miejsce w całej stawce;
    2. zawodnik starszej kategorii, jeśli na miejscu tuż przed nim jest zawodnik najmłodszej kategorii, dostaje wynik tamtego zawodnika minus 1 pkt;
    3. zawodnik starszej kategorii w każdym innym przypadku dostaje punkty EVF + 1 pkt. Dotyczy to sytuacji, gdy na miejscu tuż przed nim jest zawodnik starszej kategorii, oraz gdy zajął 1. miejsce;
    4. nowe zawodnik starszej kategorii, która liczy co najmniej 4 zawodników, dostaje co najmniej tyle punktów, ile dostałby w turnieju samej swojej kategorii, plus 1 pkt. Jeśli wtedy miałby tyle samo punktów co zawodnik, który zajął miejsce przed nim, albo więcej, wynik tamtego zawodnika podnosi się do 1 pkt więcej niż jego; tak samo dalej w górę listy. Taka stawka powstaje tylko z wyjątku od zasady łączenia 5.
  4. Wynik mnoży się przez współczynnik rangi zawodów (PPW 1,0; MPW 1,2).

Punkty EVF = 50 − 49 · ln p / ln N + 10 · liczba wygranych rund eliminacji + premia za podium (3 / 2 / 1 · 3 · ∛N).

Order of calculation: 3a–3c from the top down, where "that fencer's score" in 3b is their plain EVF from 3a; then the 3d minimum; then the 3d raises from the bottom up. After the joining change below, a bracket of 1–3 fencers appears only when the whole weapon and gender has fewer than 4 fencers; §10 asks how to score it.

2 · Zasady łączenia kategorii

Zasady łączenia
  1. Kategoria, w której startuje mniej niż 4 zawodników, jest automatycznie łączona z sąsiednią kategorią wiekową, czyli z najbliższą kategorią obecną na zawodach w tej broni i płci.
  2. W jednej stawce łączy się najwyżej 2 kategorie.
  3. Więcej niż 2 kategorie (3, 4 lub 5) łączy się tylko wtedy, gdy jest to nieuniknione, czyli gdy inaczej:
    • nie da się połączyć wszystkich kategorii poniżej 4 zawodników, albo
    • stawka miałaby mniej niż 4 zawodników.
  4. Kategoria z co najmniej 4 zawodnikami może startować osobno.
  5. Kategoria z co najmniej 4 zawodnikami może być w stawce łączonej tylko najmłodszą kategorią.

    nowe Wyjątek od zasady 5. Jeśli najmłodsza kategoria obecna w tej broni i płci ma mniej niż 4 zawodników i nawet razem z kolejnymi kategoriami poniżej 4 zawodników nie osiąga 4, łączy się je z najbliższą starszą kategorią, także gdy ma ona 4 lub więcej zawodników. Punkty w takiej stawce liczy się z punktem 3d.

  6. nowe Jeśli w całej broni i płci startuje mniej niż 4 zawodników, wszystkie obecne kategorie tworzą jedną stawkę.

When several groupings are possible, the check prefers, in this order:

  1. fewer brackets below 4 fencers;
  2. fewer uses of the exception;
  3. fewer categories beyond two per bracket;
  4. pairing small categories with each other rather than with a category of 4+;
  5. more separate brackets.

A missing category is skipped when finding a neighbour. A present category is never skipped. In history, every use of the exception has exactly the shape the wording describes: the small categories at the young end, then the next category up.

Where the cost falls: only on a youngest category of 4+.

  • A youngest category of 4+ is below its own bracket in 40.2% of its results, when older fencers place ahead of it. The exception never involves such a category, so 3d never applies to it.
  • A youngest category of 1–3 is never below its own bracket, and level with it 17.5% of the time, when it finishes last.
  • In the brackets the exception forms, the youngest category is small and can only be raised.

4 · Why the exception needs clause 3d

No scoring rule keeps Z1, Z2 and Z3 together here

Take three V0 fencers ahead of four V1 fencers, YYYOOOO (example F, real twice). The V1 fencer in 4th is 1st of 4 in V1, and their own bracket would pay 84.29.

  • Z2 needs them above 84.29.
  • Z3 needs the V0 fencers in 2nd and 3rd above them.
  • Z1 gives those V0 fencers plain EVF: 64.02 and 38.07.

One of the three has to give way whenever an older category has 4+ fencers. There are three ways out:

  1. Clause 3dproposed: keep Z2 and Z3, and raise the V0 fencers ahead: 87.29 and 86.29. The youngest category gains; nobody loses.
  2. Accept the loss: keep plain EVF for the young. Older fencers of 4+ then end below their own bracket in 29.9% of their results in these 24 brackets, which breaks Z2.
  3. No exception: the small V0 fences alone and scores by the table, which you ruled out.

Only 3d keeps both of your hard rules and never uses the table.

Considered and not proposed: skipping over a category

A second exception was tested: a small category may skip over a category of 4+ that fences alone, to reach another small category. It forms 14 brackets such as V0 with V4 while V1–V3 fence alone, and it needs the older-4+ exception only 11 times instead of 25. Youngest results raised by 3d fall from 12.7 to 5.0 over three seasons. The table count is the same 20.

It is not proposed. It breaks rule 1, the nearest category. It needs two exceptions instead of one. And it puts the youngest and oldest categories on one list when the categories between them are present.

9 · The bad sides that remain

What the numbers show
  1. The score depends on who is one place ahead. Two older fencers at neighbouring places can be far apart, depending on whether the fencer one place ahead is from the youngest category (example A: 97.00 in 2nd, 41.11 in 3rd).
  2. Only the fencer directly behind a youngest fencer gets the big lift; the others get +1 (example B: 64.67, then 28.33).
  3. Premiums are large and follow the gap to the place above, not need. Older fencers get 8.2 points over EVF on average, up to 73.2.
  4. A youngest category of 4+ ends below its own bracket in 40.2% of its results. This is by design: Z2 lets only the youngest pay.
  5. 3d raises young fencers because of how an older category did. In example F, the V0 fencer in 3rd goes from 38.07 to 86.29 because the V1 winner is 4th. This happens in 3.6% of youngest results in the real brackets, only in exception brackets.
  6. 3d brings back a chain. One 3d minimum can raise several fencers above it (example F raises two). This happens only in exception brackets; everywhere else the rule has no chains.
  7. A joined bracket of 16+ scores plain EVF with no 3d. Under the proposal this happens once in three seasons: PPW3-2025-2026 Sabre M, V0 1 + V1 2 + V2 12 + V3 2 = 17. There, a V2 fencer placed behind a younger fencer can score below their own bracket.
  8. Up to five age categories share one list (5 brackets in three seasons). That is an organiser's and safety question.
  9. The whole joined bracket must be scored together, because the fencer one place ahead can be in another category, and 3d can reach up the whole list.
What it does well
  • No small category fences alone. The table is left only where the whole weapon and gender has 1–3 fencers.
  • Every older fencer gains in every joined bracket of 4–15, including the older categories of 4+ that the exception brings in.
  • Z3 and Z4 hold everywhere; the youngest category is never below plain EVF.
  • Joining always takes the nearest present category and never skips one.
  • Short to explain: four scoring clauses, five joining rules, one exception.

10 · Question

A whole weapon and gender has only 1–3 fencers: how to score it?

This happened 20 times in three seasons, for 40 fencers, all in women's foil and sabre (list in §5). No joining within the weapon and gender can reach 4.

  1. Keep the table only for this caserecommended: „Tabela 3 / 2 / 1 stosuje się tylko wtedy, gdy w całej broni i płci startuje mniej niż 4 zawodników.” Why:
    • there is nobody to join, so the table is not a result of the joining rules;
    • EVF has no value for a single fencer (ln 1 = 0 in the formula);
    • EVF would pay 71.34 for winning among 2 and 82.98 among 3, almost the 84.29 for winning among 4. The smallest events would count nearly as much as real brackets.
  2. EVF for 2–3 fencers: 71.34 / 8.56 for N = 2 and 82.98 / 37.74 / 5.33 for N = 3. A single fencer still needs its own value, so the table does not disappear completely.
  3. Join women with men of the same weapon. This removes the table completely. It needs a separate decision on mixed-gender bouts, and a rule for reading each gender's ranking from a mixed list.
