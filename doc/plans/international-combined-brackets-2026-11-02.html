Reading an international bracket that joins two categories

An FTL bracket that joins two veteran categories refuses its whole event today. Four EVF circuit events of the last 30 months had one. EVF itself publishes each such bracket as separate categories, each with its own places and N. This plan proposes how our reader takes them, before an open event has one.

Why

One joined bracket stops an event

The international reader files every result under its bracket’s own category and derives none from birth years (ADR-105). A bracket without exactly one V0–V4 category is a SourceRefused of kind combined (international_sources._refuse_uncategorised). Every bracket is checked before anything is written, so the whole event is refused: at an open event, not one of its brackets would reach CERT or PROD while the joined one is listed.

What the reading shows

  • EVF ranks a joined bracket per category. Every one of these categories has its own N and its own places, 1, 2, 3, 3, 5…: two bronzes per category, so EVF’s places are not a slice of the joined bracket’s.
  • By EVF’s country column, no result of ours is missing from the three brackets PROD lacks. Identity can admit a fencer of ours entered under another country (ADR-106), so the proof below plans them against our roster.
  • The reader already reads EVF’s API. An event with no results link reads evf:<EVF event id> (event_sources, ADR-119 Q3 A). But no EVF event in PROD’s mirror has id_evf_event set: 0 of 67 across 2023/24–2026/27.
  • Unknown until G63 is solved: what the FTL bracket itself holds — one direct-elimination table for both categories, or joined pools and a table per category. FTL needs the pipeline account’s sign-in, which FTL refuses today.

Decide this first

Five decisions, each with a recommendation

Once C1–C5 are signed

What it changes

  1. Read the four FTL brackets, read only, once G63 is solved: what each holds, and whether its schedule row says Finished. If FTL’s bracket turns out to hold a table per category, this plan is revised before any code.
  2. Tests first, red before green (python/tests/test_combined_brackets.py, COMBINED.*):
    • a joined FTL bracket at an EVF event reads EVF’s two competitions, with EVF’s places and N;
    • EVF not yet published: the joined categories are skipped as not final, and the event stays IN_PROGRESS;
    • no EVF event found, or two: refused as today, with the reason;
    • a category EVF does not publish holds nothing and is reported;
    • an FIE event’s joined bracket is refused as today;
    • recorded EVF responses for the four events as fixtures, with no name in a log line.
  3. The reader: international_sources replaces a joined bracket at an EVF event by EVF’s competitions for its categories, recorded in the run as an evf:<id> source, so promote’s plan and the daily close read the same thing (ADR-119 §1). Its listing hash covers EVF’s rows.
  4. The re-read: each of the four leaves KNOWN_REFUSALS as soon as the reader takes it.
  5. Proof, read only against LOCAL: Jabłonna 2026 reads equal to PROD. The other three plan foreign fencers only (“no fencer of ours”), or name for you the result PROD lacks.
  6. Documents: an ADR-105 amendment, the RTM, Appendix C, the ingestion and organizers-and-engines handbook pages.

Boundaries

What this plan does not touch

  • Domestic combined pools and joint pools (ADR-049, ADR-056): split by birth year, unchanged.
  • Engarde, 4fence, Ophardt and d’Artagnan: no joined bracket seen; their readers are unchanged.
  • The identity rule and every stored result: nothing is re-ingested without your order (C5).
